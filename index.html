Do Heterogeneous Graph Neural Networks Improve Cross-Cohort Glioma Grading? A TCGA-CGGA Benchmark with Graph Ablation and Explainability

Journal
Network Modeling Analysis in Health Informatics and Bioinformatics
Publisher
Springer Nature
DOI
[to be added]
Read abstract

Glioma grading models often achieve strong internal performance but fail to maintain accuracy across independent cohorts, limiting their clinical reliability. This study evaluated whether heterogeneous graph neural networks provide predictive or interpretability advantages for binary glioma grade classification using genomic mutation and clinical features. We used 837 TCGA patients for training, validation, and internal testing, and 284 CGGA patients as an independent external cohort. A heterogeneous graph was constructed using patient and gene nodes connected through mutation-based gene-patient edges, patient-patient topology, and gene-gene co-occurrence edges. Seven heterogeneous GNN architectures were evaluated across four class-balancing strategies, including no balancing, SMOTE, CTGAN, and random oversampling. Hyperparameters were optimized using Optuna, and performance was compared against tuned tabular baselines using AUC-ROC, F1-score, grade-stratified recall, bootstrap confidence intervals, DeLong testing, and McNemar analysis. MOGAT with CTGAN achieved the highest TCGA test AUC of 0.9203, while RGCN with CTGAN achieved the highest CGGA AUC of 0.7853. However, tuned tabular baselines matched or exceeded GNN performance, with MLP reaching 0.9307 AUC on TCGA and Random Forest reaching 0.7960 AUC on CGGA. Edge-type ablation showed that gene-patient mutation edges carried most of the discriminative signal, while patient-patient topology could reduce external transfer. Explainability analyses identified IDH1, IDH2, and age at diagnosis as major predictors, consistent with established glioma biology. These findings suggest that heterogeneous GNNs provide useful structural interpretability but no consistent predictive advantage over strong tabular models for low-dimensional glioma genomic panels. Further multi-cohort validation, calibration analysis, and prospective evaluation are required before clinical deployment.

TeaBD-HybridNet: Maximizing Pretrained Knowledge Transfer in a Dual-Branch CNN-ViT Architecture for Tea Leaf Disease Classification

Conference
ICRPSET 2026: International Conference on Recent Progresses in Science, Engineering and Technology
Host
University of Rajshahi, Bangladesh
DOI
[to be added]
Read abstract

Tea leaf disease and pest identification are important for crop management. Existing hybrid CNN–Vision Transformer (ViT) approaches often perform worse than fully pretrained single-backbone models because their large randomly initialized fusion components remain undertrained on small agricultural datasets. We propose TeaBD-HybridNet, a dual-branch architecture that retains as many pretrained parameters as possible. It transplants the complete 12-block pretrained ViT depth alongside a fully pretrained CNN backbone. The two branches are fused through simple channel concatenation and a lightweight classification head. We evaluated it on the teaLeafBD dataset (5,276 images, seven classes) against ten baselines drawn from pure CNN, pure ViT, existing CNN–ViT hybrid, and state-space model families. TeaBD-HybridNet achieved 95.11% accuracy and 94.17% macro-F1. It exceeds the strongest baseline (Swin-Tiny) by 0.65 points and the strongest hybrid baseline (MobileViT-S) by 2.16 points. We also measured robustness against five real-world image corruptions and three white-box adversarial attacks and applied adversarial training as a defense. The results support the design principle of the model. Minimizing randomly initialized capacity while maximizing transferred pretrained knowledge is an effective strategy for hybrid architectures on limited agricultural datasets.

Heterogeneous Lesion Graph Network for Diabetic Retinopathy Grading: Modeling Spatial Lesion Interactions from Fundus Images

Journal
International Journal of Integrated Engineering (IJIE)
Status
Under review
Read abstract

Current automated Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) grading systems use CNNs that discard spatial relationships between retinal lesions. We propose the Heterogeneous Lesion Graph Network (HGLN), which models four lesion types (Microaneurysms, Haemorrhages, Hard Exudates, and Soft Exudates) as distinct nodes in a spatial proximity graph with 16 typed edge relations, processed via Graph Attention Networks v2 (GATv2). On the IDRiD dataset (516 images), HGLN achieved a Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK) of 0.853, outperforming a homogeneous GNN baseline by 6.4%. On external validation (APTOS 2019, 3,662 images), HGLN achieved a QWK of 0.751, surpassing ResNet-50 (0.722). Ablation studies confirm that heterogeneous typing and spatial topology are critical, while attention weight visualizations provide clinically auditable, lesion-level interpretability.

Towards Sustainable Transportation: Hybrid Vehicles as a Sustainable Alternative to Traditional Fuel Only Vehicles

Conference
7th IEOM Bangladesh International Conference on Industrial Engineering and Operations Management
Published
December 2024, IEOM Society International
DOI
10.46254/BA07.20240184
Read abstract

Use of sustainable hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs) has become essential to reduce fuel dependency as fossil fuels are limited for us. The mentioned vehicles are new energy vehicles, which are hybrid electric vehicles/plug-in electric vehicles (PHEVs), electric batteries (BEVs), and fuel cell vehicles (FCVs). The purpose of this paper is the development of more efficient hybrid electric vehicles and a definite solution to current mobility issues. Identification of environmental, economic, and technological impacts as well as potential for sustainability in using hybrid vehicles instead of the traditional fuel-powered vehicles. This is an exploratory study of the exploration by dependence on fossil fuels and increase in energy efficiency through hybrid technology with their feasibility, market acceptance, and all progress towards global sustainability goals. The research conducted a quantitative research method for collecting data based on an online survey of candidates across Bangladesh. The survey was conducted online, focusing on diverse demographics to ensure inclusivity across age, gender and income levels. The survey contains both closed and open-ended types of questions on attitudes, public awareness, and fondness concerning hybrid vehicles. The collected data was analyzed to recognize the trends and key factors influencing the adoption of hybrid vehicles in Bangladesh. The study shows that around 70%-80% respondents are expected to consider hybrid vehicles as advance in transportation alternation. Furthermore 70%-75% may favor them over traditional fuel vehicles. 60%-65% people are expected to show interest to invest in premium models because of fuel efficiency benefits. The research shows that the majority of the respondents have support for hybrid vehicles as a sustainable, practical alternative to traditional fuel-powered vehicles. Respondents realize the advantages such as societal impact, fuel efficiency and economic savings. As well as promotions, government aid, and raising awareness can speed up the adoption of hybrid vehicles.
